BS 7671:2018+A2:2022 — IET Wiring Regulations, 18th Edition
Summary of Key Requirements for UK Electricians

PART 1: SCOPE, OBJECT AND FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLES

1.1 Scope
BS 7671 applies to the design, erection, and verification of electrical installations. It also applies to additions and alterations to existing installations.

1.2 Object
The object of BS 7671 is to provide rules for the design, erection, and verification of electrical installations so as to provide for safety and proper functioning for the intended use.

PART 4: PROTECTION FOR SAFETY

Chapter 41: Protection Against Electric Shock

Regulation 411.3.3 — Automatic Disconnection of Supply
Where protective equipotential bonding is applied in accordance with Regulation 411.3.1.2, the maximum disconnection times stated in Table 41.1 shall apply for final circuits not exceeding 63 A with one or more socket-outlets, and final circuits not exceeding 32 A supplying only fixed connected current-using equipment.

Table 41.1 Maximum disconnection times:
- 50V < Uo ≤ 120V: AC systems 0.8s, DC systems 5s
- 120V < Uo ≤ 230V: AC systems 0.4s, DC systems 5s
- 230V < Uo ≤ 400V: AC systems 0.2s, DC systems 0.4s
- Uo > 400V: AC systems 0.1s, DC systems 0.1s

Regulation 411.4 — Additional Protection by RCDs
For socket-outlets with a rated current not exceeding 32 A, additional protection shall be provided by an RCD with a rated residual operating current (IΔn) not exceeding 30 mA.

For mobile equipment with a rated current not exceeding 32 A for use outdoors, additional protection shall be provided by an RCD with a rated residual operating current not exceeding 30 mA.

Chapter 42: Protection Against Thermal Effects

Regulation 421.1 — Fire Protection
Electrical equipment shall be selected and erected so that its temperature in normal operation and any foreseeable temperature rise shall not cause a fire hazard to adjacent materials.

Chapter 43: Protection Against Overcurrent

Regulation 432.1 — Nature of Protective Devices
Every circuit shall be provided with a means of protection against overcurrent. The protective device may be a fuse, a circuit-breaker, or a combination of both.

PART 5: SELECTION AND ERECTION OF EQUIPMENT

Chapter 52: Selection and Erection of Wiring Systems

Regulation 521.1 — Types of Wiring System
The types of wiring system include:
- Cables and conductors in conduit
- Cables and conductors in trunking
- Cables clipped direct
- Cables in cable tray or cable ladder
- Cables in cable ducting

Regulation 522.6 — Cables in Walls and Partitions
Where a cable is installed in a wall or partition at a depth of less than 50 mm from a surface of the wall or partition, the cable shall:
(i) incorporate an earthed metallic covering, or
(ii) be enclosed in earthed conduit, or
(iii) be enclosed in earthed trunking, or
(iv) be protected by a 30 mA RCD, or
(v) be installed in a zone within 150 mm of the top of the wall or partition, or within 150 mm of an angle formed by two adjoining walls or partitions

Chapter 54: Earthing Arrangements and Protective Conductors

Regulation 542.1 — Earthing Arrangements
The main earthing terminal shall be connected to the means of earthing by an earthing conductor.

Table 54.7 Minimum cross-sectional area of protective conductors:
- If S ≤ 16 mm²: protective conductor = S mm²
- If 16 < S ≤ 35 mm²: protective conductor = 16 mm²
- If S > 35 mm²: protective conductor = S/2 mm²

PART 6: INSPECTION AND TESTING

Chapter 61: Initial Verification

Regulation 611.1 — General
Every electrical installation shall, during erection and on completion before being put into service, be inspected and tested to verify, so far as is reasonably practicable, that the requirements of BS 7671 have been met.

Regulation 612.1 — Continuity of Protective Conductors
A test shall be made to verify the continuity of protective conductors, including protective bonding conductors.

Regulation 612.2 — Continuity of Ring Final Circuit Conductors
A test shall be made to verify the continuity of each conductor of every ring final circuit.

Regulation 612.3 — Insulation Resistance
The insulation resistance shall be measured between:
(i) each live conductor and Earth
(ii) between live conductors

Minimum values of insulation resistance:
- SELV and PELV circuits: 0.5 MΩ (test voltage 250V DC)
- Up to and including 500V (except SELV and PELV): 1.0 MΩ (test voltage 500V DC)
- Above 500V: 1.0 MΩ (test voltage 1000V DC)

Regulation 612.4 — Protection by SELV, PELV or Electrical Separation
Where protection by SELV, PELV or electrical separation is used, a measurement of the separation of the live parts from those of other circuits and from Earth shall be made.

Regulation 612.5 — Polarity
A test of polarity shall be made to verify that:
(i) every fuse and single-pole control and protective device is connected in the line conductor only
(ii) centre-contact bayonet and Edison screw lampholders have the outer or screwed contacts connected to the neutral conductor
(iii) wiring has been correctly connected to socket-outlets and similar accessories

Regulation 612.6 — Earth Fault Loop Impedance
Where protective measures are used which require a knowledge of earth fault loop impedance, the relevant impedances shall be measured or determined by an alternative method.

Regulation 612.9 — Functional Testing
Assembly of switchgear and controlgear, drives, controls, and interlocks shall be subjected to a functional test to show that they are properly mounted, adjusted, and installed in accordance with BS 7671.

PART P OF THE BUILDING REGULATIONS (ENGLAND & WALES)

Part P requires that electrical installation work in dwellings is designed and installed to protect persons from fire and injury. It applies to:
- New dwellings
- Additions and alterations to existing dwellings
- Common parts of buildings serving one or more dwellings

Notifiable work includes:
- Installation of a new circuit
- Consumer unit replacement
- Work in special locations (bathrooms, swimming pools)
- Work in kitchens that involves a new circuit

Non-notifiable work includes:
- Replacing accessories (sockets, switches, light fittings)
- Adding fused spurs to existing circuits (not in special locations)
- Like-for-like cable replacement

Competent Person Schemes:
Electricians registered with a competent person scheme (e.g., NICEIC, NAPIT, ELECSA, ECA) can self-certify their work without involving Building Control.
